Frequency vs Pitch

The physical measurement of a wave versus how your brain perceives it.

Sound is just pressure changes in the air. But what makes a bass guitar sound "low" and a whistle sound "high"? Explore the bridge between objective physics and human perception.

The Physics: Frequency

Frequency is an objective, measurable property of a wave. It is the number of complete wave cycles that pass a specific point in one second.

Hz
Unit: Hertz (Hz)
1 Hz = 1 cycle per second

The Perception: Pitch

Pitch is a subjective psychological sensation. It is how the human ear and brain interpret the frequency of a sound wave. High frequency feels like high pitch.

🧠
Determined by Biology
Depends on cochlea anatomy & auditory cortex

Key Differences

Frequency (Objective)

  • Physical property of sound waves.
  • Measured mathematically in Hertz (Hz).
  • Independent of human hearing.
  • Can be measured by oscilloscopes.
  • Determined entirely by the sound source.

Pitch (Subjective)

  • Psychological sensation in the brain.
  • Described qualitatively (high, low, flat).
  • Depends entirely on the auditory system.
  • Cannot be measured by instruments alone.
  • Can be affected by age, biology, and context (e.g., perfect pitch).

🧠 Interesting Fact: The Missing Fundamental

Your brain is so good at perceiving pitch that if you play a complex sound containing frequencies of 200Hz, 300Hz, and 400Hz, your brain will "hear" a pitch corresponding to 100Hz (the fundamental), even though the 100Hz physical frequency is completely absent from the air!

⚠️ Common Mistake

People often confuse Pitch with Loudness. A bass guitar playing a low note extremely loudly is still producing a low frequency/pitch. A mosquito buzzing quietly is producing a high frequency/pitch. Amplitude (height) ≠ Frequency (width).
