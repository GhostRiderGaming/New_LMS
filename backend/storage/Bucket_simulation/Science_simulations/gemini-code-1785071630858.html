Amplitude vs Loudness

Uncover the journey of sound. Amplitude is the physics of the wave. Loudness is the perception in your brain.

Amplitude (Objective Physics)

The measurable, physical height of a sound wave. It represents the maximum displacement of particles from their rest position and dictates the total energy carried by the wave.

Loudness (Subjective Biology)

How the human brain perceives that physical energy. While higher amplitude generally means a louder sound, our ears do not interpret sound linearly—it's a complex biological sensation.

The Acoustic System

Before a sound becomes "loud," it travels through a physical and biological chain.

Receiver (Tympanic Membrane)

The pressure wave strikes the eardrum, forcing it to vibrate at the same amplitude.

4

Processor (Brain Perception)

Mechanical vibrations become electrical signals. The brain interprets high energy as "Loud".

Did you know?

The Decibel (dB) scale used for loudness is logarithmic. A sound that is 10 dB higher has 10 times the physical amplitude/energy, but to human ears, it only sounds about twice as loud.

Common Mistake

"Pitch is the same as Loudness."
False! Pitch (high vs low note) is determined by Frequency (how fast the wave vibrates). Loudness is determined by Amplitude (how tall/forceful the wave is).
