Fermentation Process

Life without oxygen. Discover how cells extract energy when they can't breathe, creating the foundation for bread, wine, and muscle endurance.

The Cellular Factory

When oxygen is absent, the mitochondria shut down. The cell must rely entirely on the cytoplasm to produce energy through glycolysis and fermentation.

1

Glucose (6-Carbon)

The primary sugar molecule. Acts as the initial fuel source entering the cytoplasm.

2

Pyruvate (3-Carbon)

The result of splitting Glucose in half during Glycolysis. Toxic if it builds up.

3

ATP & NADH

Energy carriers. ATP is usable energy. NADH carries high-energy electrons (hydrogen).

4

Mitochondria (Inactive)

Without Oxygen, this efficient "powerhouse" cannot process Pyruvate. Fermentation takes over.

Real World Applications

🍞

Baking

Yeast performs alcoholic fermentation. The CO₂ gas gets trapped in gluten networks, making dough rise. The alcohol bakes off.

🏃‍♂️

Muscle Fatigue

During intense sprints, blood can't deliver O₂ fast enough. Muscles use lactic acid fermentation to keep producing a little ATP.

🧀

Yogurt & Cheese

Bacteria (Lactobacillus) perform lactic acid fermentation in milk. The acid lowers pH, curdling proteins and preserving the food.

⚠️ Common Misconception

"Fermentation produces a lot of energy."

False. Aerobic respiration produces ~36 ATP per glucose. Fermentation produces only 2 ATP. Its primary purpose isn't energy creation, but rather recycling NAD+ so glycolysis can continue running.
