The Physics of Refraction

Discover how light bends, slows down, and shapes our visual reality.

An Illusion of Light

Have you ever noticed a straw looking broken in a glass of water? Or heat waves shimmering over a hot road? This isn't an optical trick of the brain—it's physics in action.

Refraction is the bending of a wave when it enters a medium where its speed is different. When light passes from air (where it moves fast) into water or glass (where it moves slower), it changes direction.

What is Refractive Index (n)?

It is a dimensionless number describing how fast light travels through the material. Higher 'n' means slower light.

Real-World Applications

Lenses & Vision

Eyeglasses, microscopes, and cameras use curved glass to carefully refract light, focusing images precisely onto retinas or sensors.

Prisms & Dispersion

Because different colors (wavelengths) of light slow down by slightly different amounts, a prism refracts them at different angles, creating a rainbow.

Fiber Optics

Using "Total Internal Reflection" (when light tries to enter a faster medium at a steep angle and reflects completely), fiber cables bounce light across oceans instantly.
