The Invisible Universe

Explore the complex machinery of microorganisms.

Microorganisms (microbes) are microscopic life forms. They comprise the majority of biomass on Earth and regulate biogeochemical cycles.

Diversity of Microbes

Bacteria

Prokaryotic, single-celled organisms. Found everywhere from deep sea vents to the human gut.

Viruses

Acellular entities. They consist of genetic material (DNA/RNA) enclosed in a protein coat and require a host to replicate.

Fungi (Yeast)

Eukaryotic organisms. Includes yeasts and molds. Crucial for decomposition and fermentation processes.

Applications & Real-World Impact

Biotechnology & Medicine

Microbes are bio-factories. By inserting human genes into bacterial plasmids, we use bacteria to mass-produce vital human hormones like Insulin for diabetics, completely replacing animal-derived sources.

The Human Microbiome

You are more microbe than human. There are roughly 39 trillion bacterial cells in/on your body compared to 30 trillion human cells. They digest complex carbs, synthesize vitamins (like K2), and train our immune system.

Nitrogen Fixation

Plants need Nitrogen to grow, but cannot use atmospheric N2. Specialized bacteria (like Rhizobium) live in plant roots and "fix" atmospheric nitrogen into ammonia, forming the base of the entire global food chain.

Common Misconception

"All bacteria are bad and cause disease."
False. Less than 1% of known bacteria cause illness in humans (pathogens). The vast majority are harmless or highly beneficial to the environment and our health.
