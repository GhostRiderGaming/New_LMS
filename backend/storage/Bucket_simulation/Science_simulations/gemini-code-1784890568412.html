The Four Elements

A healthy compost pile requires a specific balance of four main components to keep microorganisms active.

1
Carbon (Browns) Provides energy for microbes. Includes dry leaves, twigs, cardboard, and straw.
2
Nitrogen (Greens) Provides proteins for microbial growth. Includes grass clippings, vegetable scraps, and coffee grounds.
3
Moisture Microbes need a damp environment (like a wrung-out sponge) to survive and move.
4
Oxygen (Aeration) Aerobic bacteria require air to rapidly break down material without creating bad odors.

The Decomposition Cycle

Self-Heating Physics

The heat in a compost pile (up to 70°C/160°F) comes entirely from the metabolic energy of billions of microbes respiring, not from the sun.

Soil Structure

Humus (finished compost) acts like a sponge. It can hold up to 200% of its weight in water, making soil drought-resistant.

Common Mistake: Meat & Dairy

Never add animal products or oils to a standard backyard bin. They undergo putrefaction (anaerobic rotting), produce foul odors, and attract pests.
