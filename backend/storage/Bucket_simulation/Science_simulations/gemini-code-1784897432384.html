Oxidation & Reduction

Explore the invisible transfer of electrons that powers batteries, rusts metal, and sustains life.

The Core Concept

Redox reactions are simply about the movement of electrons between atoms. They always happen together; if one loses, another must gain.

O.I.L. R.I.G.

Oxidation Is Loss • Reduction Is Gain (of electrons)

Oxidation

An atom loses electrons, becoming positively charged (or less negative).

Reduction

An atom gains electrons, becoming neutrally or negatively charged.

The Galvanic Cell Setup

A classic example of redox at work is a battery (Galvanic cell). It separates the oxidation and reduction reactions to force electrons to travel through a wire, creating usable electricity.

Salt Bridge

A tube filled with inert ions (like $K^+$ and $NO_3^-$). It completes the circuit by allowing ions to flow, neutralizing charge buildup.

5

Voltmeter / Load

Measures the electrical potential difference (voltage) or consumes the electrical energy (like a bulb).

Real-world Applications

  • Batteries: From AAs to Lithium-ion EV batteries, all store energy via redox.
  • Corrosion: Rusting iron is spontaneous oxidation.
  • Biology: Cellular respiration (making ATP) relies heavily on electron transport chains.

Did you know?

The term "oxidation" originally meant "combining with oxygen" (like fire or rust). Later, scientists realized oxygen simply steals electrons, so they redefined oxidation as the loss of electrons, whether oxygen is involved or not!

Common Mistake

Current vs. Electron Flow:
In physics, "conventional current" flows from Positive (+) to Negative (-). However, the actual physical particles (electrons) flow from the Anode (-) to the Cathode (+). They move in opposite directions!
