Newton's First Law

The Law of Inertia. Objects are lazy. They want to keep doing exactly what they are already doing.

Begin Exploration ↓

Two States of Existence

Unless acted upon by an unbalanced force, an object will remain in one of these two states indefinitely.

Objects at Rest

If velocity is zero, it stays zero. A heavy object on a table will sit there for millions of years until something pushes or pulls it.

Objects in Motion

If moving, it keeps moving in a straight line at the exact same speed. In the vacuum of space, a thrown wrench travels forever.

The Anatomy of Inertia

Three main physical factors determine how an object behaves under Newton's First Law.

1
2
3
  • 1

    Mass (Inertia)

    The amount of matter. More mass means more resistance to changing speed or direction.

  • 2

    Net Force

    An unbalanced push or pull. It is the ONLY thing that can overcome inertia and change velocity.

  • 3

    Friction

    The "hidden" opposing force. On Earth, it constantly slows things down, hiding the true nature of inertia.

Real-World Applications

Where Newton's First Law impacts daily life.

Seatbelts in Cars

When a car crashes and stops suddenly, your body wants to stay in motion at the original speed. The seatbelt provides the unbalanced force to stop you safely.

Washing Machines

During the spin cycle, the drum pushes inward on the clothes to keep them moving in a circle, but the water droplets fly straight out through the holes due to inertia.

Common Mistake

People often think force is required to keep an object moving. It isn't! Force is only required to overcome friction. In a vacuum, it coasts forever with zero force.
