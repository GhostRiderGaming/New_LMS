Physical Separation Technique

The Art of Sieving

Sieving is a simple yet powerful method to separate particles based purely on their physical size. By passing a mixture through a woven mesh, smaller particles fall through while larger ones are retained.

Anatomy of a Sieve

A standard laboratory or industrial sieve consists of a few crucial components designed for precise separation.

1
2

1

Rigid Frame

Typically made of stainless steel or brass. It holds the mesh taut and provides structural integrity during vigorous shaking.

2

Woven Wire Mesh

The active separation layer. Precision woven wires create uniform openings across the entire surface area.

3

Pore Size (Aperture)

The exact distance between adjacent wires. This defines the "cut-off" size: smaller particles pass, larger ones stay.

Ensuring uniform powder sizes before compressing them into tablets.

Facts & Pitfalls

Did you know?

The standardized measurement for mesh size is often given in "mesh numbers" (e.g., 100 mesh), which represents the number of openings per linear inch. A higher mesh number means smaller holes!

Common Mistake: Blinding

Overloading the sieve or using damp materials causes particles to wedge into the pores, blocking them. This is called "blinding" and halts separation.
