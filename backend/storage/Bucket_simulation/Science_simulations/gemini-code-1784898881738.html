What is a Homologous Series?

Imagine building a chain with Lego bricks. You start with a specific head and tail, and to make it longer, you keep adding the exact same middle block.

In chemistry, a Homologous Series is a family of organic compounds that follow a specific pattern:

  • They share the same General Formula.
  • They have the same Functional Group (determining chemical properties).
  • Successive members differ by a -CH₂- unit (mass of 14u).
  • Their physical properties (like boiling point) change gradually as the chain grows.

🔥 Gradation in Physical Properties

As the chain gets longer, the molecule gets heavier and surface area increases. This increases Van der Waals forces between molecules, requiring more energy to pull them apart.

Result: Melting and Boiling points rise predictably.

🧪 Similar Chemical Properties

Because every member of a series has the same functional group (e.g., all alcohols have -OH), they undergo the exact same chemical reactions.

Example: All alcohols combust to form CO₂ and H₂O.

⛽ Real World Applications

The Alkane series fuels our world, separated by boiling point in refineries:

  • n=1-4: Gases (Heating, Stoves)
  • n=5-8: Liquids (Gasoline/Petrol)
  • n=9-16: Heavy Liquids (Diesel, Kerosene)
  • n>20: Solids (Paraffin Wax, Asphalt)
