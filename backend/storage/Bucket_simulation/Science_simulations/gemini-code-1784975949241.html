The Food Web

Discover the invisible threads of energy that connect all living things. Not just chains, but a complex, fragile, and beautiful network of survival.

Chain vs. Web

A Food Chain is a simple, straight line showing who eats whom. (e.g., Grass → Rabbit → Fox).

But nature isn't simple. A Fox eats rabbits, but also birds and mice. A Food Web combines many interconnected food chains to show the realistic, tangled pathways of energy in an ecosystem.

Mechanism: The Flow of Energy

Energy doesn't cycle; it flows in one direction and dissipates. This is governed by the 10% Rule: only about 10% of energy is passed to the next level. The rest is used for living or lost as heat.

Stage 1: Solar Input

The cycle begins. The Sun continuously beams electromagnetic energy toward Earth. This is the ultimate source of power for almost all ecosystems.

Out: Photons

Stage 2: Photosynthesis

Producers capture a tiny fraction of solar energy. They use it to convert carbon dioxide and water into glucose (chemical energy) stored in their leaves.

In: Sunlight Out: Chemical Energy (100%)

Stage 3: Primary Consumption

A herbivore eats the plant. Here the 10% Rule applies heavily: 90% of the energy the plant made was used for its own growth or lost as heat. Only 10% is passed to the herbivore.

In: 10,000 Joules Out: 1,000 Joules Stored, 9,000 Lost as Heat

Stage 4: Secondary Consumption

A carnivore hunts the herbivore. Again, the herbivore burned most of its energy moving, breathing, and keeping warm. The carnivore only extracts 10% of what the herbivore had.

In: 1,000 Joules Out: 100 Joules Stored, 900 Lost

Stage 5: Apex Predation

The top predator eats the carnivore. By this stage, the available energy is extremely small. This is why food chains rarely exceed 4 or 5 levels, and why apex predators are rare.

In: 100 Joules Out: 10 Joules Stored (0.1% of original)

⚡

Keystone Species

Some species have a disproportionately large effect on their environment. If a keystone species (like sea otters or wolves) is removed, the entire food web can collapse.

🌍

Real-world Application

Conservationists use food web models to predict what will happen if they introduce a new species to an area, or if a pesticide wipes out a specific insect population.

⚠️

Common Mistake

Thinking arrows in a food web point to who is being eaten. WRONG! Arrows point in the direction of energy flow. (From the prey, TO the predator's mouth).

Quick Revision

A Food Web is an interconnected network of food chains.
Energy originates from the Sun.
Producers make food. Consumers eat it.
Only 10% of energy transfers up each trophic level.
Arrows show the direction of energy flow.
