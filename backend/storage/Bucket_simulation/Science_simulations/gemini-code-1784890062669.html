Real-World Applications

🚆 Maglev Trains

Uses the principle of magnetic repulsion to levitate trains above tracks, eliminating friction and allowing speeds over 600 km/h.

🔊 Audio Speakers

An electromagnet rapidly switches polarity, interacting with a permanent magnet to push and pull a cone, creating sound waves.

🏥 MRI Scanners

Uses massive superconducting magnets to align protons in the human body, generating detailed internal medical images safely.

Common Misconceptions

❌ Breaking a magnet isolates poles

Reality: If you snap a bar magnet in half, you do not get a standalone North and South. You get two smaller complete magnets, each with its own N and S poles.

❌ All metals are magnetic

Reality: Only ferromagnetic materials (Iron, Nickel, Cobalt, and some rare earth alloys) interact strongly with magnets. Metals like Aluminum, Copper, and Gold do not.

❌ Magnetic fields block gravity

Reality: Magnetic levitation counteracts gravity locally through an opposing force, but it does not "turn off" gravity in that space.
