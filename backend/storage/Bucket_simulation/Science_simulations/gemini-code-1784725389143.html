The Cumulonimbus Engine

A severe thunderstorm is not just rain; it's a massive thermodynamic engine. Towering up to 10 miles high, violent updrafts and downdrafts of air collide, stripping electrons from ice crystals and creating a massive electrical imbalance.

This immense atmospheric battery must eventually discharge, seeking the path of least resistance to the ground.

Anatomy of a Strike

Lightning is not instantaneous. It is a rapid, step-by-step sequence of plasma channels connecting sky and earth.

Charge Separation

Mechanism Details
Ice collisions build (-) charge at cloud base.

Inside the turbulent cloud, updrafts push ice crystals up, colliding with heavier graupel (soft hail) falling down. Electrons are stripped, resulting in a massive negative charge pooling at the bottom of the cloud, inducing a positive charge on the ground below.

Fact vs Fiction

Hotter than the Sun

A lightning channel reaches temperatures up to 30,000°C (54,000°F). That is roughly five times hotter than the surface of the sun.

The 30/30 Rule

If the time between flash and thunder is 30 seconds or less, go inside. Stay inside for 30 minutes after hearing the last thunder.

Upward Strikes

While rare, tall structures like the Empire State Building can trigger upward lightning. The building launches a streamer to meet the cloud.

Myth

Rubber tires on a car protect you from lightning strikes.

Truth: It's the metal frame (Faraday Cage effect) that routes the current around you to the ground. Convertibles or fiberglass cars offer no protection.

Myth

Lightning never strikes the same place twice.

Truth: Lightning often strikes the same place repeatedly, especially tall, pointed, isolated objects.
