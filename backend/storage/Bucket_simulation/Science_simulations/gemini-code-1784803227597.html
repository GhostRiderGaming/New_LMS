System Anatomy

Explore the specialized components that transform roof runoff into usable stored water.

Component Key

  • 1

    Catchment Area

    The surface (usually a roof) that directly receives rainfall and provides water to the system.

  • 2

    Conveyance (Gutters)

    Channels and pipes that transport harvested water from the catchment to the storage area.

  • 3

    First Flush Diverter

    Valve system that routes the initial, dirtiest runoff away from the main tank to ensure quality.

  • 4

    Filtration Unit

    Layers of gravel, sand, and charcoal that remove suspended pollutants and biological matter.

  • 5

    Storage Tank

    Vessel (underground or above-ground) sized to hold filtered water until it is needed.

Knowledge Base

The 1mm Rule

1 millimeter of rain over 1 square meter of roof yields exactly 1 liter of water. It's a perfect 1:1 mathematical relationship before evaporation losses.

Groundwater Recharge

Not all systems store water in tanks. Many route filtered runoff directly into recharge pits or borewells to replenish depleted underground aquifers.

Skipping First Flush

The most common failure is omitting the first flush diverter. The first 10 minutes of rain wash bird droppings and dust off the roof; without diversion, this contaminates the entire tank.
