The Electromagnet

Magnetism born from electricity. Discover how flowing electrons can create powerful, controllable magnetic fields that drive modern technology.

System Components

An electromagnet requires three primary components to function. Hover over the markers to identify them.

1
2
3
  • 1

    Power Source (DC)

    Provides the voltage that pushes electrons through the circuit. Usually a battery.

  • 2

    Conductive Coil (Solenoid)

    Insulated copper wire wrapped in tight loops. Coiling concentrates the magnetic field lines.

  • 3

    Ferromagnetic Core

    Soft iron inserted inside the coil. Its internal magnetic domains align with the coil's field, amplifying it thousands of times.

Beyond the Basics

🚅

Maglev Trains

Magnetic levitation trains use superconducting electromagnets to repel the track, floating above it to eliminate friction and reach speeds over 600 km/h.

🔊

Loudspeakers

Every speaker contains an electromagnet (voice coil) suspended near a permanent magnet. Varying audio current causes the coil to rapidly attract and repel, vibrating the cone to create sound.

⚠️

Common Mistake

"Electromagnets are permanent."
False. Their defining feature is that they can be turned on and off instantly by controlling the electric current. If power drops, the magnetism stops.

🏥

MRI Machines

Magnetic Resonance Imaging uses massive electromagnets cooled by liquid helium. The field is so strong (thousands of times Earth's magnetic field) it aligns protons inside your body to create detailed 3D images.
