A solar power plant is a sequence of highly efficient energy conversion stages. Explore the macro components below.

1

Photovoltaic Array

Thousands of solar panels capture photons from sunlight and convert them directly into Direct Current (DC) electricity.

2

Central Inverter

Converts the raw, variable DC power from the panels into synchronized Alternating Current (AC) required by the electrical grid.

3

Step-Up Transformer

Increases the AC voltage significantly (e.g., from 400V to 33,000V+) to minimize energy loss during long-distance transmission.

4

Transmission Grid

High-voltage lines distribute the generated clean electricity across large distances to substations and eventually homes.

Space Origins

Photovoltaic technology was initially developed to power satellites in space. Vanguard 1, launched in 1958, was the first satellite to use solar cells.

⚠️

Common Misconception

Solar panels don't need direct heat to work—in fact, high temperatures actually decrease a solar panel's electrical efficiency. They run best on bright, cold days.

🔄

Alternate Tech (CSP)

Unlike PV which uses light directly, Concentrated Solar Power (CSP) uses mirrors to focus heat, boiling water to spin a traditional turbine.
