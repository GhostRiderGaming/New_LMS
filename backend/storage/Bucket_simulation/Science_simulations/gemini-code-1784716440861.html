The Chemistry of Change

During adolescence, microscopic chemical messengers rewrite the body and brain. Discover the HPG Axis—the biological control system behind puberty.

The Endocrine System

The network of glands that produce and release hormones. Hover or tap the markers to explore the core components.

1

Hypothalamus

The brain's control center. It kicks off puberty by releasing pulses of GnRH (Gonadotropin-releasing hormone).

2

Pituitary Gland

The master gland. Triggered by the hypothalamus, it releases LH and FSH into the bloodstream.

3

Adrenal Glands

Located above the kidneys, they produce androgens responsible for early changes like body hair and body odor.

4

Gonads (Ovaries / Testes)

The target factories. They receive signals from the pituitary to produce Estrogen or Testosterone, driving physical maturation.

Mechanism: The HPG Axis

A continuous chain reaction of chemical signals flowing from the brain to the body.

1. The Trigger

The hypothalamus matures and begins firing pulses of GnRH.

Fascinating Facts & Common Myths

Brain Remodeling

During adolescence, the brain undergoes "synaptic pruning," deleting unused neural pathways to become faster and more efficient.

Myth: Hormones = Crazy

While hormones affect mood, intense emotions are mostly due to the amygdala (emotion center) developing faster than the prefrontal cortex (logic center).

Sleep Cycle Shift

Melatonin (the sleep hormone) is released later at night in teenagers, making them naturally want to sleep later and wake up later.
