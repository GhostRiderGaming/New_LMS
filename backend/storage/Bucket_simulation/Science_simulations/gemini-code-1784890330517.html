Molecular Breakdown

Nitrogen

Diatomic Molecule (N₂)

Bond: Triple Covalent
Reactivity: Very Low (Inert)
Role: Prevents rapid burning

Oxygen

Diatomic Molecule (O₂)

Bond: Double Covalent
Reactivity: High (Oxidizer)
Role: Cellular energy extraction

Carbon Dioxide

Linear Molecule (CO₂)

Structure: O = C = O
Density: Heavier than air
Role: Plant food, heat trap

Observation

At sea level, gravity pulls gas molecules tightly together, resulting in high density and pressure. You are breathing 78% N₂ and 21% O₂.

Practical Applications

Liquid Nitrogen

Cooled to -196°C, it's used in medicine to freeze warts and in culinary arts for flash-freezing.

Argon in Bulbs

Because it's inert, Argon is pumped into incandescent bulbs to stop the tungsten filament from burning up.

CO₂ Extinguishers

Heavier than oxygen, CO₂ blankets a fire, starving it of the O₂ needed for combustion.

Common Misconceptions

"We breathe mostly Oxygen"

False. Every breath you take is 78% Nitrogen. You only use a fraction of the 21% Oxygen inhaled.

"Air is weightless"

False. A standard room contains about 50-70 kg of air. Atmospheric pressure is literally the weight of air above you.
