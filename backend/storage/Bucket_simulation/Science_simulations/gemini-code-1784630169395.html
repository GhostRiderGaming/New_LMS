Coalification

The 300-million-year transformation of prehistoric forests into fossilized energy.

Coal is not just a rock; it is stored solar energy from the Carboniferous period. Plants captured sunlight through photosynthesis, died in swamps where oxygen was scarce, and were buried. Over geological time, immense heat and pressure chemically altered this organic matter, driving out water and concentrating pure carbon.

Analysis & Context

🌲

Why doesn't coal form today?

During the Carboniferous period, organisms that could digest lignin (wood fiber) hadn't evolved yet. Today, fungi and bacteria decompose dead trees before they can accumulate deep enough to form coal.

⚡

Energy Density

As coal ranks increase (from Peat to Anthracite), moisture and volatile matter are expelled, leaving behind a higher percentage of pure carbon. This means Anthracite burns hotter and cleaner per kilogram than Lignite.

⚠️

Common Mistake

People often think coal comes from dinosaurs. False. Coal forms almost entirely from terrestrial plant matter (trees, ferns). Oil and natural gas generally form from marine organisms (plankton, algae).

Quick Revision Summary

Plants → Peat → Lignite → Bituminous → Anthracite

Process driven by: Absence of Oxygen + Geological Time + Lithostatic Pressure + Geothermal Heat
