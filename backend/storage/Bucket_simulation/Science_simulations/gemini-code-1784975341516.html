Electromagnetism Principle

Fleming's Left-Hand Rule

A simple way to predict the direction of force generated when a current flows through a magnetic field. The fundamental principle behind electric motors.

The FBI Mnemonic

Hold your left hand so the Thumb, Index, and Middle fingers are at right angles (mutually perpendicular) to each other.

The Motor Effect Mechanism

How a magnetic field and an electric current interact to create physical motion (a linear sequence).

Stage 1: Magnetic Field Setup

A strong permanent magnet provides a uniform magnetic field (B) flowing from North to South.

Applications

  • Electric Motors: The primary application. Electrical energy converts to mechanical motion.
  • Loudspeakers: Varying current in a magnetic field causes a cone to vibrate, creating sound.
  • Galvanometers: Measures small electrical currents via deflection.
🧠

Common Mistakes

  • Using the Right Hand instead of Left. (Right hand is for generators/induction).
  • Pointing current (I) in the direction of electron flow. Always use conventional current (+ to -).
  • Not keeping fingers at 90 degrees to each other.
💡

Quick Fact

Discovered by John Ambrose Fleming in the late 19th century as a simple mnemonic for his students at University College London to easily understand the motor effect.
