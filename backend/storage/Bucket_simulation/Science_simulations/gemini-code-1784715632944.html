Water Treatment Plant

The invisible infrastructure that sustains cities. Discover how civil engineering, chemistry, and fluid dynamics combine to transform contaminated raw water into crystal-clear, safe drinking water through a continuous, multi-stage purification process.

01. Anatomy of a Plant

Explore the primary infrastructure. Select a marker to understand its function in the sequence.

1

Intake & Screens

Draws raw water from source. Large screens block fish, debris, and logs.

2

Flocculation Basin

Chemicals are added and gently mixed. Dirt particles stick together to form heavy "floc".

3

Sedimentation Tank

Water flow slows down. Heavy floc particles sink to the bottom as sludge.

4

Multimedia Filter

Water trickles through anthracite coal, sand, and gravel, trapping microscopic impurities.

5

Disinfection

Chlorine, Ozone, or UV light is applied to neutralize bacteria and viruses.

04. Knowledge Base

💧

Did you know?

Ozone gas ($O_3$) is often used instead of chlorine for disinfection. It's much faster, leaves no chemical taste, and breaks down back into oxygen naturally.

🏙️

Scale of Operation

A typical municipal plant processes anywhere from 10 million to over 1 billion gallons of water per day, requiring massive concrete basins and constant flow.

❌

Common Misconception

"Filters remove everything." False. Physical filters remove particles and some microbes, but dissolved chemicals and heavy metals require advanced reverse osmosis or chemical precipitation.
