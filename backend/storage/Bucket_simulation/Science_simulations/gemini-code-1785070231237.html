Newton's Third Law

"For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."

Forces never exist alone. They always occur in pairs applied to two different interacting objects.

Everyday Applications

Walking

When you walk, your foot pushes backward against the ground (Action). The ground pushes your foot forward with equal force (Reaction).

Helicopters

The rotors push a massive volume of air downward (Action). The air pushes back upward on the rotors, creating lift (Reaction).

Firearms (Recoil)

Expanding gas pushes the bullet rapidly forward out the barrel (Action). The gas simultaneously pushes the gun backward into the shooter's shoulder (Reaction).

Common Mistake

"If action and reaction are equal and opposite, shouldn't they cancel out, resulting in no motion?"

Correction: They do not cancel out because they act on different objects. The skater pushes the wall; the wall pushes the skater. You only calculate the net force on one object at a time to find its acceleration.

Fascinating Fact

When you drop a pen, gravity pulls the pen down toward the Earth (Action). According to the third law, the pen exerts an equal gravitational force pulling the entire Earth upward (Reaction)! The Earth's mass is just so massive that its resulting acceleration is unmeasurably small.
