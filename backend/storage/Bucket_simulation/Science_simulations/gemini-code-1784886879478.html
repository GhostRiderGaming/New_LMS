The Testing Process

A generalized linear sequence of how chemical food tests are conducted in a laboratory.

1. Preparation: Solid food is crushed with water to form an extract.
2. Reagent Addition: Specific chemical drops are added to the sample.
3. Reaction: A chemical change occurs, altering the liquid's color.
4. Result: The specific color confirms the presence of the nutrient.

Knowledge Base

The Iodine Complex

Why does starch turn blue-black? Starch is coiled like a spring (amylose helix). Iodine molecules fit perfectly inside this coil, altering how light is absorbed and creating an intense dark color.

Biuret Mechanism

The Biuret test detects peptide bonds (the links holding proteins together). Copper(II) ions in the reagent form a complex with nitrogen atoms in these bonds, shifting the color from blue to violet.

Common Mistakes

In the Ethanol Emulsion test, students often forget to add water after dissolving the food in ethanol. Fats dissolve in ethanol but not in water. Adding water forces the fat out of solution, forming the visible cloudy emulsion.
