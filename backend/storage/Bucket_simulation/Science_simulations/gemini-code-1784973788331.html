The Geometry of Light

When light passes through a transparent medium with curved surfaces, such as a lens, it bends. This bending is called refraction.

By tracing the path of specific geometric rays from an object through a lens, we can perfectly predict where an image will form, whether it will be upside down, and how large it will appear.

This principle is the foundation of cameras, telescopes, microscopes, and the human eye.

Fascinating Fact

"The lens in your eye is flexible. Small muscles constantly change its curvature (focal length) to keep objects in focus as they move closer or further away—a process called accommodation."

The 3 Rules of Ray Tracing

To locate an image, we only need to trace two of these three standard rays originating from the tip of the object. The point where they intersect (or appear to intersect) is where the image forms.

1. The Parallel Ray

A ray parallel to the principal axis passes through the Focus (F) after refraction.

Real-World Applications

Magnifying Glass

Uses a convex lens. When the object is placed *between* the Optical Centre and Focus (u < f), it forms a virtual, erect, and highly magnified image.

Human Eye & Glasses

The eye contains a natural convex lens. Myopia (shortsightedness) is corrected using concave lenses to diverge light slightly before it enters the eye.

Cameras

Use a convex lens system to form a real, inverted, and diminished image directly onto the digital sensor (or film) located precisely at the image distance (v).

Quick Revision & Mistakes

Common Pitfalls

  • Sign Convention: Forgetting that Object Distance (u) is always taken as negative (measured to the left).
  • Virtual vs Real: Real images are formed by actual intersection of rays (solid lines). Virtual images are formed by extrapolation (dashed lines).
  • Concave Focal Length: A concave lens has a negative focal length in equations because its principal focus is virtual (on the left side).

The Lens Formula

1/f = 1/v - 1/u

f = focal length, v = image distance, u = object distance
