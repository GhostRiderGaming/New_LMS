Phase Transition

Evaporation

The invisible escape of water molecules into the atmosphere, happening quietly around us every single day.

What is Evaporation?

Evaporation is a type of vaporization that occurs on the surface of a liquid as it changes into the gas phase. Unlike boiling, which happens throughout the entire liquid body at a specific temperature, evaporation occurs gradually and can happen at any temperature above absolute zero.

It is the primary pathway that water moves from the liquid state back into the water cycle as atmospheric water vapor.

Real-World Applications

Sweating (Cooling)

When sweat evaporates from your skin, it takes thermal energy (heat) with it, effectively lowering your body temperature.

The Water Cycle

Oceans and lakes evaporate continuously. This invisible vapor rises, cools, condenses into clouds, and returns as rain.

Drying Clothes

Hanging clothes on a line uses wind to sweep away humid air and solar heat to increase molecule energy, speeding up evaporation.

Interesting Fact

Evaporative Cooling

Evaporation is inherently a cooling process. Because only the highest-energy (hottest) molecules manage to escape the liquid, the average energy of the molecules left behind decreases. This makes the remaining liquid physically colder!

Common Mistake

"Water only turns to gas at 100°C"

Many confuse boiling with evaporation.

Correction: Boiling happens at the boiling point (100°C for water) throughout the liquid. Evaporation happens only at the surface, and occurs at ANY temperature above freezing. Puddles dry up on a 15°C day!

Quick Revision

  • Definition: Liquid turning to gas at the surface below boiling point.
  • Mechanism: High-energy molecules overcome intermolecular forces and escape into the air.
  • Rate increases with: Higher Temperature, larger Surface Area, higher Wind Speed.
  • Rate decreases with: Higher ambient Humidity (air is already full of water vapor).
  • Result: Causes cooling of the remaining liquid body.
