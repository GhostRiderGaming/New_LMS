Key Differences

Feature Manure (Organic) Fertilizer (Synthetic)
Nutrient Concentration Relatively low and variable High and precisely known
Action Speed Slow (needs microbial breakdown) Immediate (water soluble)
Soil Impact Improves structure & water retention No structural benefit, can increase salinity
Environmental Risk Low (except if over-applied near water) High risk of leaching and algal blooms
Cost (Large Scale) Bulky to transport, low material cost Expensive, but easy to transport/apply
💡

Scientific Fact

Synthetic fertilizers revolutionized global agriculture during the "Green Revolution." It is estimated that without the Haber-Bosch process (which synthesizes ammonia for fertilizer from atmospheric nitrogen), the Earth could only support about half of its current human population.

⚠️

Common Mistake

"Fertilizer Burn" occurs when synthetic fertilizers are over-applied. Because they are concentrated salts, excess amounts draw moisture out of the plant roots through osmosis, dehydrating and killing the plant instead of feeding it.
