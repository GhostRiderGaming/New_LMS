Seed Dispersal

Nature's ingenious engineering to ensure survival, minimize competition, and conquer new territories.

Why Do Plants Scatter Their Seeds?

The Problem with Proximity

If seeds fall directly beneath the parent plant, they enter a brutal battle for survival. They must compete with their own massive parent for sunlight, water, and soil nutrients.

Dispersal acts as an evolutionary escape pod, carrying the next generation to fertile, unoccupied land.

Dispersal Agents & Anatomy

Plants have evolved specialized anatomical structures to exploit different environmental forces for transport.

Pappus (Parachute)

Fine, feathery bristles that catch air currents, acting like an aerodynamic drag device to slow descent.

2

Beak (Stalk)

An extension separating the parachute from the seed mass, lowering the center of gravity for stable flight.

3

Achene (Seed payload)

The heavy, nutrient-dense core containing the embryo, covered in tiny barbs to anchor into soil upon landing.

1

Exocarp (Outer Skin)

A smooth, waterproof outer layer that prevents saltwater from penetrating and rotting the seed inside.

2

Mesocarp (Fibrous Husk)

Thick layer of woven fibers trapping countless air pockets. Acts as a highly buoyant life jacket.

3

Endosperm (Nutrient Reserve)

The rich liquid and solid "meat" that provides enough energy for the seedling to establish itself on a barren sandy beach.

1

Micro-Hooks (Barbs)

Stiff, curved extensions that act like velcro, instantly tangling into passing animal fur or human clothing.

2

Protective Casing

A hard outer shell protecting the seed from being crushed while attached to a heavy, moving animal.

Fascinating Applications & Facts

The Invention of Velcro

In 1941, Swiss engineer George de Mestral went hunting with his dog. He noticed burdock seeds covered in tiny hooks clinging to his dog's fur and his pants. Under a microscope, he saw the hook-and-loop mechanism and invented Velcro, revolutionizing fasteners worldwide.

Explosive Autochory

The Sandbox tree (Hura crepitans) builds internal pressure in its seed pods as they dry. When ripe, they explode with a loud crack, firing seeds at speeds up to 70 meters per second (160 mph). This mechanism relies purely on physical tension, no animals required.

Quick Revision

  • Anemochory (Wind): Needs light weight, wings (samaras), or parachutes (pappus).
  • Hydrochory (Water): Needs waterproof coats and buoyant, air-trapping fibrous layers.
  • Zoochory (Animal): Needs hooks/barbs for external transport, or fleshy sweet fruit for internal transport.
  • Goal: Reduce competition with parent plant and colonize new space.
