Why is a Blood Moon Red?

During a total lunar eclipse, Earth blocks direct sunlight. However, Earth's atmosphere bends (refracts) sunlight, scattering away blue light and letting red light pass through into the umbra, painting the Moon red. It's the light of all Earth's sunrises and sunsets simultaneously.

❌

Common Misconception

"Eclipses should happen every month."
They don't because the Moon's orbit is tilted about 5 degrees relative to Earth's orbit around the Sun. Most months, the Moon's shadow passes above or below Earth (New Moon), and Earth's shadow misses the Moon (Full Moon).

🔭

Scientific Application

The 1919 Total Solar Eclipse allowed Arthur Eddington to observe starlight bending around the Sun's mass. This provided the first experimental proof of Albert Einstein's Theory of General Relativity.

📏

The Cosmic Coincidence

Total solar eclipses are possible only because of a perfect geometric coincidence: The Sun is about 400 times wider than the Moon, but it is also exactly 400 times farther away, making them appear the exact same size in our sky.
