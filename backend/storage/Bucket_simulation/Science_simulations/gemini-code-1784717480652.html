The 300-Foot Vertical Lift

Giant Redwoods lift water over 300 feet entirely against gravity without moving parts. It works through Cohesion-Tension—as water evaporates from leaves, it literally pulls the chain of water molecules below it all the way from the roots.

⇄

Pressure-Flow Hypothesis

Unlike xylem (which is just physics), phloem requires energy (ATP). The plant actively pumps sugar into phloem cells. Water follows via osmosis, creating high pressure that physically pushes the sap to areas of low pressure (roots/fruits).

Common Misconceptions

❌ Misconception

"Plants suck water up like a straw using their roots."

✅ Reality

Root pressure only pushes water up a few inches. 99% of the heavy lifting is done by evaporation at the top (transpiration) pulling the water column upwards.

❌ Misconception

"Sap only flows downwards."

✅ Reality

Phloem sap moves bidirectionally. In spring, before leaves form, sap moves upwards from root storage to fuel new bud growth (this is how we get Maple Syrup!).
