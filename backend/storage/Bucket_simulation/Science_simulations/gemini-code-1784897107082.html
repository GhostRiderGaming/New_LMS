Common Mistakes

  • Changing subscripts: Changing H₂O to H₂O₂ to balance oxygen changes water into hydrogen peroxide. Never change small numbers!
  • Fractions: While sometimes used in advanced chemistry, standard balancing requires whole numbers (integers).
  • Forgetting to multiply all: A coefficient of '2' in front of H₂O means 4 Hydrogen AND 2 Oxygen.

💡
Pro Tips

  • Save lonely elements for last: If an element appears by itself (like O₂ or Fe), balance it at the very end.
  • Polyatomic Ion trick: If a group like (SO₄) appears on both sides, treat it as one giant atom rather than counting S and O separately.
  • The Even/Odd rule: If you have an odd number of atoms on one side and an even on the other, multiply the odd side by 2 to make it even.

🌍
Real World Fact

The Haber Process (N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃) shown in our simulation is responsible for synthesizing fertilizer that feeds nearly half of the global population today. Balancing that single equation literally changed human history and won a Nobel Prize.
