The Fire Triangle (Prerequisites)

Combustion requires three elements to be present simultaneously. Remove any one, and the reaction stops.

1
2
3
  • 1

    Fuel

    Combustible material (solid, liquid, or gas) containing carbon-hydrogen bonds to be broken.

  • 2

    Oxygen

    The oxidizer. Air contains ~21% oxygen. Higher concentrations lead to faster, hotter reactions.

  • 3

    Heat (Activation Energy)

    Initial energy required to raise the fuel to its ignition temperature and start the reaction.

💡 Real-world Application

Internal Combustion Engines (Cars)

Modern vehicles use controlled, repetitive explosive combustion. Fuel injectors spray a fine mist of petrol (increasing surface area), spark plugs provide activation energy, and the resulting mini-explosion pushes pistons to generate motion.

⚠️ Common Misconception

"All fire produces soot and smoke."

False! Soot is unburnt carbon. When oxygen supply is perfect (Complete Combustion), natural gas burns with a blue flame producing only invisible Carbon Dioxide and Water vapor. Smoke indicates Incomplete Combustion.

Quick Summary

Combustion is a chemical reaction of a fuel with oxygen yielding heat. Rapid = fast with external heat. Spontaneous = starts on its own at room temp. Explosive = massive sudden gas/sound release. Complete = Blue flame, high O₂, no soot. Incomplete = Yellow flame, low O₂, produces soot.
