Petroleum Fractional Distillation

Explore how crude oil is separated into valuable fuels and chemicals using temperature gradients and phase changes.

1. Components & Architecture

Hover or tap the numbered markers on the diagram to identify components and understand their function.

1

Furnace (Heater)

Heats incoming crude oil to roughly 400°C, turning most of it into a mixture of hot vapors and liquid.

2

Fractionating Column

A tall steel tower where separation occurs. It acts as a massive heat exchanger.

3

Bubble Cap Trays

Perforated horizontal plates. Rising vapors bubble through liquid on the trays, facilitating heat exchange and condensation.

4

Temperature Gradient

The core principle. The tower is very hot at the base and progressively cooler towards the top.

5

Extraction Pipes

Draw off the condensed liquids (fractions) at specific heights corresponding to their boiling points.

4. The Fractions

The distinct groups of hydrocarbons separated during the process.

Refinery Gas

< 20°C
Chain: C1 - C4 (Shortest) State: Gas at room temp Use: Heating, cooking (LPG)

Petrol / Gasoline

20 - 150°C
Chain: C5 - C10 Flow: Highly volatile, runny Use: Fuel for cars

Naphtha

150 - 200°C
Chain: C7 - C14 Flow: Volatile liquid Use: Chemical feedstock, plastics

Kerosene / Paraffin

200 - 300°C
Chain: C11 - C15 Flow: Less flammable liquid Use: Jet engine fuel, lamps

Diesel Oil

300 - 370°C
Chain: C15 - C20 Flow: Viscous liquid Use: Fuel for trucks, trains

Fuel Oil

370 - 400°C
Chain: C20 - C30 Flow: Thick, dark liquid Use: Ships, factory boilers

Residue / Bitumen

> 400°C
Chain: > C30 (Longest) Flow: Solid/extremely thick Use: Road surfacing, roofing

💡 Core Principle

Different hydrocarbon chains have different boiling points due to intermolecular forces (Van der Waals forces). Larger molecules have stronger forces and require more heat energy to break apart and turn into vapor.

⚠️ Common Mistake

Students often think distillation creates new substances via chemical reaction. It does not. It is purely a physical sorting process based on boiling points. The molecules themselves remain unchanged.
