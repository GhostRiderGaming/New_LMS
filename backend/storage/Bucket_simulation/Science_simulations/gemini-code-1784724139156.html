1. Solar Insolation

Sunlight strikes the equator directly, delivering highly concentrated energy. At the poles, the same energy spreads over a larger curved area, resulting in cooler temperatures.

Interesting Facts

  • Antarctica is technically a desert. It receives less than 50mm of precipitation annually.
  • The Atacama Desert in Chile is so dry that some weather stations have never recorded a single drop of rain.
  • Ocean currents can drastically alter climate; the Gulf Stream keeps Northern Europe much warmer than similar latitudes in Canada.
❌

Common Mistakes

  • Weather vs. Climate: Weather is short-term (rain today). Climate is long-term averages (30+ years).
  • Equator = Hottest? Not always. The hottest temperatures on Earth are actually recorded in subtropical deserts (around 30° latitude), not the equator, due to lack of cloud cover.
📝

Quick Revision

  • Latitude: Dictates solar intensity angle.
  • Altitude: Temperature drops ~6.5°C per 1km rise (Lapse Rate).
  • Continentality: Oceans moderate temperatures and provide moisture; deep inland areas have extreme seasons.
