The Microscopic Secret: Domains

Inside materials, atoms act like tiny magnets. These group into clusters called Magnetic Domains. The difference between materials lies purely in how these domains behave.

Magnetic Material

e.g., Iron, Cobalt, Nickel

Domains easily align in the same direction, creating a strong combined magnetic force.

Non-Magnetic Material

e.g., Wood, Plastic, Gold

Domains are rigidly locked in random directions. Their individual forces cancel each other out entirely.

How Magnetization Works

Watch how a piece of unmagnetized iron becomes temporarily magnetic when brought near a permanent magnet.

1

Unmagnetized State

Iron's internal domains point randomly. Net magnetic force is zero.

2

Field Introduction

A permanent magnet approaches, casting its magnetic field over the iron.

3

Domain Alignment

The external field forces the iron's domains to rotate and align with it.

4

Induced Magnetism

Fully aligned domains turn the iron into a temporary magnet, causing attraction.

Applications & Facts

Maglev Trains

Using superconducting electromagnets, these trains levitate above the tracks, eliminating friction and reaching speeds over 600 km/h.

Earth's Shield

Earth has a liquid iron core generating a massive magnetic field that protects us from lethal solar wind and cosmic radiation.

Not all metals are magnetic

A common mistake is thinking all metals attract magnets. Gold, silver, copper, and aluminum are entirely non-magnetic!
