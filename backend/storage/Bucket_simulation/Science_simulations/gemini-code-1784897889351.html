The Reactivity Series

A chemical hierarchy determining which metals will displace others in a reaction. Higher metals are more eager to lose electrons.

Practical Applications

🛡️

Galvanization

Iron ships and pipes are coated with Zinc. Because Zinc is higher in the series, it reacts with oxygen/water first, "sacrificing" itself to prevent the iron underneath from rusting.

🔋

Batteries

The voltage of a battery depends on the distance between the two metals in the reactivity series. Zinc and Copper make a weak battery, while Lithium (very high) makes powerful ones.

Common Mistakes

❌

Reversing the Logic

A common error is thinking Copper will displace Zinc from Zinc Sulfate. Remember: The solid metal must be higher on the list than the metal in the solution.

❌

Ignoring Hydrogen

Metals above Hydrogen can displace Hydrogen gas from acids (bubbles!). Metals below Hydrogen (Copper, Silver, Gold) do not react with standard dilute acids.
