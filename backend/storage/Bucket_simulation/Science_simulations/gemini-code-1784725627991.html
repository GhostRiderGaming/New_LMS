Horizons Legend

1

O Horizon (Organic)

Leaf litter, humus, and decomposing plant matter.

2

A Horizon (Topsoil)

Mineral soil mixed with organic matter. Most biologically active.

3

E Horizon (Eluviation)

Leached layer, pale and sandy, depleted of clay and minerals.

4

B Horizon (Subsoil)

Zone of accumulation (illuviation) for minerals leached from above.

5

C Horizon (Parent Material)

Weathered rock fragments with little biological activity.

6

R Horizon (Bedrock)

Unweathered solid rock mass at the base of the profile.

The Hydrologic Cycle in Soil

Observe how water drives soil formation through a continuous vertical flow mechanism.

1. Precipitation

Rainwater falls onto the surface O Horizon.

2. Infiltration & Solution

Water enters Topsoil, dissolving organic acids and soluble minerals.

3. Eluviation (Leaching)

Water washes clays and minerals out of the E Horizon, leaving sand.

4. Illuviation (Accumulation)

Minerals precipitate and deposit into the denser B Horizon (Subsoil).

5. Percolation to Aquifer

Filtered water reaches bedrock, joining the groundwater table.

Did You Know?

It takes approximately 500 to 1,000 years to form just one inch of topsoil naturally.

Common Mistake

Dirt isn't soil. "Dirt" is dead, displaced earth. "Soil" is a living ecosystem full of microbes.
