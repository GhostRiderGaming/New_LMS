Wind and clouds don't just appear; they are driven by the unequal heating of Earth's surface and the resulting movement of air and moisture.

Primary Cloud Formations

Cumulus

Low Altitude
Puffy, cotton-like clouds with flat bases. Formed by strong localized updrafts of warm, moist air.

Stratus

Low Altitude
Flat, featureless, uniform layers covering the sky. Formed when a large mass of air rises slowly and evenly.

Cirrus

High Altitude (>20,000 ft)
Wispy, hair-like streaks. Because it is so cold at this altitude, they are made entirely of ice crystals, not water droplets.

Fascinating Fact

A single fluffy cumulus cloud can weigh over 1 million pounds (500,000 kg). How does it float? The weight is distributed among trillions of microscopic water droplets spread across a massive volume, and the warm updraft rising from the earth pushes against them, keeping them suspended.

Common Mistake

Myth: Clouds are made of water vapor.
Reality: Water vapor is an invisible gas. The clouds you see are liquid water droplets or solid ice crystals. When vapor cools to the "dew point", it condenses onto microscopic dust/smoke particles (aerosols) to become visible.
