The Two Architects of Light

Lenses are meticulously shaped pieces of transparent material (like glass or plastic) that manipulate light through refraction. By altering the curvature of surfaces, we can control exactly where light goes.

Convex Lens

Thicker in the middle, thinner at the edges. Also known as a Converging Lens because it bends incoming parallel light rays inward to a single focal point.

Concave Lens

Thinner in the middle, thicker at the edges. Also known as a Diverging Lens because it spreads parallel light rays outward, as if originating from a virtual point.

Real World Applications

Convex Lens (Converging) Concave Lens (Diverging)
Human Eye: The crystalline lens in our eyes is convex, focusing light onto the retina. Myopia Glasses: Used to correct short-sightedness by diverging light before it hits the eye.
Magnifying Glasses: Produces a magnified, virtual, erect image when the object is close. Flashlights / Headlights: Used to widen the beam of light emitted by the bulb.
Cameras & Telescopes: Used to gather light and focus it onto a sensor or film. Peepholes (Door viewers): Provides a wide field of view, making objects outside look smaller.
