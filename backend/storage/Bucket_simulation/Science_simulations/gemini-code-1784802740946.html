Pathways of Transfer

Pollination is classified based on the genetic relationship between the pollen source and the receptive stigma.

1. Autogamy

Strict Self-Pollination. Pollen transfers from the anther to the stigma of the exact same flower.

Result: Genetic clones. Low variation, but ensures seed set if pollinators fail. (e.g., Peas, Peanuts)

2. Geitonogamy

Neighbor Pollination. Pollen transfers to the stigma of a different flower on the same plant.

Result: Functionally cross-pollination (requires agent), but genetically identical to self-pollination.

3. Xenogamy

True Cross-Pollination. Pollen transfers to a flower on a different plant of the same species.

Result: High genetic variation, robust offspring, evolutionary advantage. (Most plants).

Vectors & Agents

Plants are stationary. To move pollen, they recruit external forces (Abiotic) or living organisms (Biotic) through complex evolutionary bribes.

Anemophily (Wind)

Flowers are usually small, unpigmented, lack nectar, and produce massive amounts of dry, lightweight pollen. Stigmas are large and feathery to catch pollen from the air.

Examples: Grasses, Maize, Pine, Oak

Entomophily (Insects)

The most common biotic method. Flowers are large, brightly colored, emit strong fragrances, and offer nectar or pollen as a reward. Pollen is sticky or spiky.

Examples: Rose, Orchid, Sunflower

Ornithophily (Birds)

Flowers are typically tubular, very strong, and vividly colored (often red or orange, invisible to most insects but bright to birds). They produce abundant dilute nectar but lack odor.

Examples: Hibiscus, Aloe, Strelitzia

Chiropterophily (Bats)

Occurs at night. Flowers are robust, bell-shaped, pale or white (visible in moonlight), and emit strong fermenting/musty odors. They produce copious nectar.

Examples: Baobab, Agave, Banana

Fascinating Facts

  • ✨ The Orchids' Deception: Ophrys orchids perfectly mimic female wasps visually and chemically. Male wasps attempt to mate with the flower, accidentally pollinating it.
  • ✨ Fig Wasp Sacrifice: Fig trees require specific wasps that crawl inside the unripe fig, lay eggs, and die. The fig digests the wasp while the seeds mature.
  • ✨ Water Skiing Pollen: In Vallisneria (Hydrophily), male flowers detach, float to the surface, and sail like tiny boats until they crash into female flowers.

Common Misconceptions

  • ❌ "Pollination = Fertilization"
    False. Pollination is merely the physical transport of pollen. Fertilization is the later fusion of gametes inside the ovary.
  • ❌ "All flowers need bees"
    False. Most staple food crops (wheat, rice, corn) are wind-pollinated and do not rely on insects at all.
  • ❌ "Self-pollination implies a single flower"
    False. Geitonogamy involves two different flowers on the same plant, yet is genetically self-pollination.
