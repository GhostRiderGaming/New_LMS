Anatomy of a Reflection

A plane mirror isn't just a piece of glass. It is a precisely engineered optical component. Hover over the markers to understand its structure.

1
Glass Sheet Transparent front layer protecting the reflecting surface.
2
Silver Coating The actual reflecting surface (usually silver or aluminum) deposited on the back of the glass.
3
Protective Paint Opaque backing that prevents scratches and oxidation of the silver layer.
4
Incident Ray The incoming ray of light striking the mirror surface.
5
Reflected Ray The ray of light bouncing off the mirror into the observer's eye.

Characteristics of the Image

↔️

Lateral Inversion

The image is flipped left-to-right. This is why AMBULANCE is written in reverse on the front of vehicles.

📏

Same Distance & Size

The object distance ($d_o$) equals the image distance ($d_i$). The image is exactly the same size as the object (magnification = 1).

👻

Virtual & Erect

The image cannot be projected onto a screen because light rays don't actually meet there. It is upright (erect), not upside down.

Common Misconceptions

The image is ON the mirror surface.

Correction: The image is formed behind the mirror. If you stand 2 meters in front, your image appears to be 2 meters behind the glass (total distance 4m).

Mirrors reverse top and bottom.

Correction: Mirrors reverse front-to-back along the z-axis (depth). When you point North at a mirror, your reflection points South. Up remains Up.

Real-World Applications

  • ➔ Periscopes: Submarines use two plane mirrors angled at 45° to see above water level.
  • ➔ Kaleidoscopes: Multiple mirrors angled towards each other create infinite symmetrical patterns.
  • ➔ Blind Corners: Sometimes used to see around tight hallway corners, though convex mirrors are preferred for a wider field of view.
