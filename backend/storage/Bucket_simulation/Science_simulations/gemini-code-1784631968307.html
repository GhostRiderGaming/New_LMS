Electroplating

The Alchemy of Electricity

Discover how electrical current can dissolve solid metal into a liquid, transport it invisibly, and reconstruct it atom by atom onto a new surface.

The Apparatus

Hover or tap the markers to understand the essential components of an electrolytic cell.

1

DC Power Supply

Provides the electrical energy. It acts as an electron pump, pulling electrons from the anode and pushing them to the cathode.

2

Anode (+)

The positive electrode, made of the metal you want to plate with (e.g., pure Copper). It dissolves into the solution during the process.

3

Cathode (-)

The negative electrode. This is the object to be plated (e.g., a steel key). Metal ions deposit here to form the coating.

4

Electrolyte Solution

A liquid containing dissolved metal ions (e.g., Copper Sulfate). It completes the circuit by allowing ions to flow between electrodes.

Mechanism of Action

Electroplating is a continuous flow process. Watch how electrical energy drives chemical changes to transport metal atom by atom.

Real-world Applications

Jewelry: Coating cheap metals with gold or silver.
Corrosion Protection: Galvanizing steel with zinc.
Electronics: Plating connectors with gold for conductivity.

⚠️

Common Mistakes

Wrong Polarity: If you reverse the battery, the object dissolves instead of getting plated!
Dirty Surface: Grease prevents atoms from bonding, causing peeling.

🧠

Quick Revision

Remember PANIC:
Positive is Anode.
Negative Is Cathode.
Electrons flow from Anode to Cathode through wires; Ions flow through liquid.
