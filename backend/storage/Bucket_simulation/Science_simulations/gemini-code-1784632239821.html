    Downdrafts

    Cold air and heavy precipitation (rain/ice) falling rapidly, pulling negative charge downwards.

  • 2

    Updrafts

    Warm, moist air rising fast, carrying lighter ice crystals upward.

  • 3

    Positive Region (Top)

    Lighter ice crystals, stripped of electrons during collisions, accumulate at the freezing cloud top.

  • 4

    Negative Region (Base)

    Heavier graupel (soft hail) gains electrons and sinks to the bottom of the cloud.

  • 5

    Ground Shadow

    The negative cloud base repels electrons in the ground deep into the earth, leaving the surface intensely positive.

Hotter Than The Sun

A lightning return stroke can heat the surrounding air to 30,000°C (54,000°F) — roughly five times hotter than the surface of the sun. This rapid heating creates the shockwave we hear as thunder.

⚡

Myth: Never Strikes Twice

Lightning frequently strikes the same place multiple times, especially tall, pointed, isolated objects. The Empire State Building is struck roughly 25 times per year.

🌱

Nature's Fertilizer

The extreme heat of lightning breaks the strong bonds of atmospheric nitrogen gas (N2), allowing it to bond with oxygen. Rain carries these nitrates to earth, acting as natural fertilizer for plants.
