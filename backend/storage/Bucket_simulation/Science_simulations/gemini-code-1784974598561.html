Clinical Optics

Refractive Errors Compared

Myopia (Nearsightedness)

Light focuses in front of the retina. Close objects are clear, distant objects are blurry.

  • Physical Cause: Eyeball is too long front-to-back, or cornea is too steeply curved.
  • Correction: Concave (minus) lens. It diverges light rays slightly before they enter the eye, pushing the focal point back onto the retina.
  • Global Trend: Rapidly increasing globally, linked to increased near-work (screens, reading) and lack of outdoor sunlight exposure during childhood.

Hyperopia (Farsightedness)

Light focuses behind the retina. Distant objects are clear, close objects are blurry.

  • Physical Cause: Eyeball is too short, or the cornea is too flat.
  • Correction: Convex (plus) lens. It converges light rays before they enter, pulling the focal point forward onto the retina.
  • Accommodation: Young people with mild hyperopia often don't notice it, because their crystalline lens works overtime to auto-focus. This causes severe eye strain and headaches.
