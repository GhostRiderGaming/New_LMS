The Work Calculator

In physics, work isn't just effort. It's the measure of energy transfer that occurs when an object is moved over a distance by an external force.

W = F × d × cos(θ)

Zero Work Orbit

Gravity constantly pulls a satellite towards Earth, but because the force (down) is at a 90° angle to its motion (forward), gravity does zero work on it. Cos(90°) = 0.

🧱

The "Pushing a Wall" Mistake

You can push a brick wall all day and get exhausted, but if the wall doesn't move (distance = 0), in physics, you have done exactly zero work.

📝

Quick Revision

Formula: W = F × d × cos(θ)
Unit: Joule (J)
Note: 1 Joule = 1 Newton-meter. Work is a scalar quantity (has magnitude, no direction, but can be negative).
