Fundamental Material Classes

Metals

Defined by metallic bonding. Excellent conductors, ductile, and strong.

Polymers

Long molecular chains. Lightweight, flexible, and excellent insulators.

Ceramics

Ionic/covalent networks. Extremely hard, heat-resistant, but brittle.

Composites

Engineered mixtures combining properties, like strength and low weight.

Fact

Aerogel, a highly porous synthetic material derived from a gel (a polymer network), is over 99% air, making it one of the best thermal insulators ever created.

Common Mistake

Confusing "hard" with "strong". Glass (ceramic) is very hard (resists scratching) but not tough (shatters easily under impact).

Application

Space shuttle tiles used porous silica ceramics. They dissipated heat so poorly that you could pick one up by the edges seconds after removing it from a 2200°F oven.

Revision

Metals = Sea of electrons. Polymers = Tangled chains. Ceramics = Rigid ionic lattices. Composites = Matrix + Reinforcement.
