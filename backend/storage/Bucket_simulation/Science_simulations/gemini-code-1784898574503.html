The Blueprint of Matter

What is a Molecule?

A molecule is an electrically neutral group of two or more atoms held together by chemical bonds. They are the smallest identifiable units into which a pure substance can be divided while still retaining its composition and chemical properties.


Think of atoms as lego bricks. A single brick is an atom. When you connect them together following specific rules, you create a molecule.

Beyond the Canvas

Carbon's Versatility

Carbon has 4 valence electrons, allowing it to form up to 4 covalent bonds. This unique geometry allows for complex chains and rings, forming the basis of all organic life and materials like diamonds and graphite.

⚠️

Common Mistake

Covalent vs Ionic: Covalent bonds (shown above) share electrons between non-metals. Ionic bonds happen when metals completely steal electrons from non-metals (like Salt: NaCl).

🧬

Macromolecules

DNA, proteins, and synthetic polymers (plastics) are just massive molecules formed by millions of covalent bonds linking smaller structural units (monomers) together into chains.
