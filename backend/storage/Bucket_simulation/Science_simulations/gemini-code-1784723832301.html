Atmospheric Physics

Automatic Weather Station

The silent sentinels decoding the Earth's atmosphere.

A modern Automatic Weather Station (AWS) is a highly accurate, self-contained laboratory. It continuously measures atmospheric conditions—converting invisible physical forces like air pressure, solar radiation, and wind kinetic energy into digital signals. These stations form the vital nervous system of global climate science and daily weather forecasting.

Anemometer

Wind Vane

Indicates wind direction. The aerodynamic tail ensures the arrow points into the wind.

3

Stevenson Screen

Shields thermistors (temp) and hygrometers (humidity) from direct sunlight while allowing airflow.

4

Tipping Bucket Rain Gauge

Measures precipitation. A small seesaw tips every 0.2mm of rain, generating an electrical pulse.

5

Datalogger & Barometer

The 'brain' that processes analog signals to digital data. Also houses pressure sensors.

6

Solar Panel & Battery

Provides autonomous power, ensuring continuous operation in remote environments.

7

Telemetry Antenna

Transmits packets of compressed meteorological data via radio or satellite to forecasters.

Applications

Where is this data used?

  • Aviation: Runway wind and pressure mapping.
  • Agriculture: Evapotranspiration rates for crop irrigation.
  • Synoptic Forecasting: Feeding supercomputers for global weather models.

Did You Know?

The Stevenson Screen (the slatted white box) was invented by Thomas Stevenson, a civil engineer and the father of famous author Robert Louis Stevenson (Treasure Island).

Common Misconception

Myth: "Wind chill" is measured by the weather station thermometer.
Truth: Thermometers only measure actual air temp. Wind chill is a calculated index of how cold it feels to human skin due to heat loss from wind.
