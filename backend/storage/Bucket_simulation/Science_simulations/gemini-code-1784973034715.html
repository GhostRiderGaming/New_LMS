Stimulus & Response

The Four Primary Tropisms

Directional growth is determined by specific environmental triggers.

Phototropism

Growth in response to light. Shoots bend towards the light source to maximize photosynthesis.

Gravitropism

Growth in response to gravity. Roots grow down (positive), shoots grow up (negative).

Thigmotropism

Growth in response to touch. Enables climbing plants and tendrils to coil around supports.

Heliotropism

Young sunflowers track the sun across the sky daily. Unlike standard phototropism which is permanent growth, this is a diurnal (daily) cycle driven by motor cells pumping potassium ions, turning the flower head.

🛰️

Space Agriculture

In microgravity on the ISS, plants get confused because gravity (geotropism) is absent. Scientists must use directional LED lighting to guide shoot growth and forced water injection to guide root growth.

⚠️

Common Misconception

Nastic vs Tropic: The Venus Flytrap snapping shut is not a tropism. It's a Nastic movement. Tropisms are directional growth depending on stimulus direction. Nastic movements happen regardless of stimulus direction.
