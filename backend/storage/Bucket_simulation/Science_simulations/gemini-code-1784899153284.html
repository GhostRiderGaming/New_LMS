The "No Pulse" Phenomenon

Because modern VADs use continuous centrifugal spinning rather than pulsatile squeezing, patients often have no palpable pulse or measurable blood pressure via standard cuffs, despite blood circulating perfectly.

Common Misconception

Myth: A VAD replaces the heart.
Fact: A VAD is an assist device. The native heart remains in the chest. The VAD simply acts as a booster pump to help a failing left ventricle do its job.

Magnetic Levitation

Inside the pump, the spinning rotor doesn't touch the walls. It floats in a magnetic field. This frictionless design prevents the grinding up of delicate red blood cells (hemolysis) and prevents mechanical wear over years of use.
