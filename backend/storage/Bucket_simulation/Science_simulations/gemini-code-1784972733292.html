2. The Action Potential

How does electricity move through biological tissue? Not like copper wire. Neurons use specialized protein channels to rapidly pump electrically charged ions (Sodium Na+ and Potassium K+) across their membrane.

This creates a moving wave of voltage change called an Action Potential. It is an "All-or-None" event.

Membrane: -70 mV
Resting State: Inside is negatively charged compared to outside. Channels are closed.

3. Synaptic Transmission

When the electrical signal reaches the end of the axon, it cannot cross the physical gap (synapse) to the next cell. It must be converted into a chemical signal.

PRE-SYNAPTIC TERMINAL
POST-SYNAPTIC DENDRITE

1 Arrival

Action potential reaches the terminal, changing local voltage.

2 Calcium Influx

Voltage-gated Calcium (Ca²⁺) channels open. Ca²⁺ rushes into the cell.

3 Fusion

Calcium causes synaptic vesicles (sacs of chemicals) to fuse with the membrane.

4 Release (Exocytosis)

Neurotransmitters are dumped into the synaptic cleft (the gap).

5 Binding

Neurotransmitters bind to receptors on the next cell, triggering a new signal.

4. Interactive: The Need for Speed

Evolution solved a problem: making signals faster without making axons massively thick. The solution is Myelin. Test how insulation affects signal velocity (Saltatory Conduction).

Unmyelinated Axon (Continuous Conduction) Speed: ~2 m/s
Myelinated Axon (Saltatory Conduction) Speed: ~120 m/s

*Diseases like Multiple Sclerosis destroy myelin, slowing signals.

Clinical & Real-World Context

💉

Medical Application

Local Anesthetics (like Lidocaine used by dentists) work by physically blocking the Voltage-Gated Sodium Channels. If Na+ cannot enter the axon, no action potential fires. The pain signal never reaches your brain.

🧠

Common Mistake

"The brain is purely electrical."
False. While signals within a neuron are electrical (action potentials), communication between neurons is almost entirely chemical (neurotransmitters). It's an electro-chemical system.

⚡

Fascinating Fact

A single neuron can fire up to 1,000 action potentials per second. The human brain contains roughly 86 billion neurons, forming over 100 trillion synaptic connections. This parallel processing dwarfs modern supercomputers.
