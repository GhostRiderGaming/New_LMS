How a Compass Works

Navigation through Earth's invisible magnetic field.

The Giant Magnet Beneath Us

A compass is essentially a tiny, lightweight magnet balanced on a nearly frictionless pivot. It works because it responds to a much larger magnet: The Earth itself.


Deep inside Earth, a churning core of liquid iron generates a massive magnetic field. This field surrounds the planet, creating invisible lines of magnetic force running generally from the South Pole to the North Pole.


Because opposites attract in magnetism, the North pole of the compass needle is attracted to Earth's Magnetic North (which, physically speaking, acts as the south pole of Earth's internal magnet). By aligning with these invisible lines, a compass provides a reliable directional reference anywhere on the surface.

Anatomy of a Baseplate Compass

A modern orienteering compass relies on several precise components working together. Explore the parts below.

1

Baseplate

Clear acrylic plate with straight edges and rulers for map work.

2

Direction of Travel Arrow

Points the way you should walk when following a bearing.

3

Housing (Bezel)

Rotatable dial marked with 360 degrees. Liquid inside dampens needle wobble.

4

Magnetic Needle

Magnetized piece of metal. The red end always seeks Earth's Magnetic North.

5

Orienting Arrow ("The Shed")

Fixed inside housing. Used to align with the needle ("Red in the Shed").

True North vs Magnetic North

Maps point to True North (Geographic North Pole). Compasses point to Magnetic North. The angle difference between them is called Declination, which varies depending on where you are on Earth.

Animal Navigators

Humans need compasses, but some animals have built-in ones. Birds, sea turtles, and even some bacteria contain magnetite in their bodies, allowing them to sense Earth's magnetic field for migration (Magnetoreception).

Common Mistake

Using a compass near a car, phone, or power lines. Local metals and electromagnetic fields easily overpower Earth's weak magnetic field, causing the needle to give completely false readings.
