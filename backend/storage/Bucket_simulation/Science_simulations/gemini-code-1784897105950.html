Types of Chemical Reactions

Explore the fundamental mechanisms that rearrange the building blocks of the universe. Understand how atoms break apart, swap partners, and fuse to create entirely new substances.

The Four Fundamental Patterns

Most chemical interactions fall into one of these structural architectures. Hover over the markers to identify the components.

1. Synthesis (Combination)

A + B → AB

Two or more simple substances combine to form a more complex product.

  • 1
    Reactant A E.g., Sodium (Na) atom.
  • 2
    Reactant B E.g., Chlorine (Cl) atom.
  • 3
    Compound AB New fused molecule (NaCl).

2. Decomposition

AB → A + B

A complex molecule breaks down into simpler constituent parts, usually requiring energy.

  • 1
    Compound AB Water (H₂O) molecule.
  • 2
    Product A Hydrogen gas.
  • 3
    Product B Oxygen gas.

3. Single Displacement

A + BC → AC + B

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound.

  • 1
    Element AActive metal (Iron).
  • 2
    Compound BCCopper Sulfate.
  • 3
    Compound ACIron Sulfate (New).
  • 4
    Element BKicked out (Copper).

Mechanism: How Bonds Break & Form

A chemical reaction isn't instant. It is a linear sequence driven by kinetics. Molecules must collide with enough force (Activation Energy) to break existing bonds before new ones can form.

Everyday Chemistry

🔥

Combustion

Car engines and campfires rely on oxygen reacting rapidly with fuel to release heat and light.

🧲

Oxidation (Rust)

A slow synthesis reaction where iron exposed to water and oxygen forms iron oxide.

💊

Neutralization

Antacid tablets (base) react with stomach acid to form harmless water and salt, curing heartburn.
