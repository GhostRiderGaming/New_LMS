Electromagnetic Induction

Creating electricity from motion and magnetism.

Discovered by Michael Faraday in 1831, it is the principle behind how almost all of the world's electricity is generated today.

Real-world Applications

Power Generators

Spinning turbines rotate massive magnets inside coils, generating the AC power that runs modern civilization.

Induction Cooktops

Alternating current in a coil creates a rapidly changing magnetic field, inducing eddy currents in the metal pot to create heat.

Guitar Pickups

Vibrating steel strings disrupt the magnetic field of permanent magnets inside the pickup, inducing a tiny audio signal in the surrounding coil.

🧠 Did you know?

The Earth's magnetic field interacts with the solar wind (moving charged particles), creating vast electrical currents in the upper atmosphere. This is the same principle of induction causing the Aurora Borealis (Northern Lights)!

⚠️ Common Mistake

"A strong magnet creates a strong current."
False! A stationary magnet, no matter how strong, induces ZERO current. It is the rate of change (movement) of the magnetic field that creates the electricity, not just its presence.

Quick Revision

  • Induction requires: A magnetic field, a conductor, and relative motion.
  • Faster motion = Higher induced voltage.
  • Reversing direction = Reverses current direction.
  • More coil turns = Higher induced voltage.
