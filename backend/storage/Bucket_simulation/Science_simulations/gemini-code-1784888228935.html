Winnowing

An ancient method of separating grain from chaff, relying entirely on the elegant interplay of aerodynamic drag and gravity.

The Physics Rule

Winnowing works because of differing terminal velocities. Drag force ($F_d = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 C_d A$) affects the low-mass, high-surface-area chaff much more than the dense grain grain, causing divergent parabolic trajectories.

Modern Application

Modern combine harvesters still use this exact principle! Inside the massive machines, mechanical fans generate controlled wind while sieves shake the crop, rapidly sorting tons of grain per hour based on mass.

Common Mistake

If the wind velocity is too high, or the drop height is too extreme, the horizontal force overcomes the grain's mass advantage. The result? Good grain is blown away with the chaff, reducing yield.
