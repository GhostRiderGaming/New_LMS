Boiling Point & Molecular Size:
Smaller molecules (like Propane, C₃H₈) have weak intermolecular forces and boil at low temperatures. Large molecules (like Bitumen, C₅₀+) have strong forces and require immense heat to vaporize.

The Fractions & Applications

The products separated by the column are the foundation of modern industry and transportation.

Refinery Gas < 20°C

Smallest molecules (1-4 Carbons). Exits as a gas. Used as bottled heating gas (LPG) and cooking fuel.

Petrol / Gasoline 20-150°C

Highly volatile and flammable liquid (5-9 Carbons). The primary fuel for standard internal combustion car engines.

Naphtha 150-200°C

Crucial chemical feedstock. Used to manufacture plastics, synthetic rubbers, and industrial solvents.

Kerosene 200-300°C

Medium-weight liquid. Used primarily as aviation fuel (Jet-A1) and in domestic heaters/lamps.

Diesel 300-370°C

Heavier, less volatile fuel. Ignites under pressure rather than a spark. Used in trucks, trains, and ships.

Fuel Oil 370-400°C

Thick, viscous liquid. Burned in power stations and used as bunker fuel for massive ocean liners.

Bitumen > 400°C

The heaviest fraction (70+ Carbons). A thick, black, sticky semi-solid. Used for surfacing roads and roofing.
