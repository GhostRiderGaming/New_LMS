Thermal Expansion

When matter absorbs heat, its particles vibrate vigorously and push each other apart, causing the material to expand in volume, area, or length.

The Bimetallic Strip

A classic application demonstrating differential thermal expansion. Two different metals bonded together expand at different rates, causing the strip to bend.

1

Brass Layer (High $\alpha$)

Has a higher coefficient of linear expansion. It wants to elongate more when heated.

2

Steel Layer (Low $\alpha$)

Has a lower coefficient. It elongates less. Because they are bonded, the structure must curve.

3

Thermal Energy

Increases the kinetic energy of atoms in both metals, causing them to push apart.

4

Deflection

The strip bends towards the metal with the lower expansion rate (steel), breaking or making electrical circuits.

Expansion Joints

Bridges and railway tracks are built with physical gaps. Without these, summer heat causes the metal/concrete to expand, buckle, and warp, leading to catastrophic failure.

Anomalous Expansion of Water

Unlike most substances, water contracts when heated from 0°C to 4°C. This unique property ensures lakes freeze from the top down, allowing aquatic life to survive winters.

Common Misconception

People often think the atoms themselves swell up when heated. False. The atoms remain the same size; it is the average distance between them that increases due to higher kinetic energy.
