The Quest for Stability

Atoms seek a stable arrangement of electrons (usually an octet in their outermost shell). Metals, having few valence electrons, readily give them up. Non-metals, lacking few, eagerly accept them. This fundamental exchange creates oppositely charged ions bound together by powerful electrostatic forces.

🔹 Properties & Applications

Crystalline Solids: Form hard, brittle lattices at room temperature.

High Melting Points: Huge amounts of energy are required to break the strong electrostatic bonds (NaCl melts at 801°C).

Conductivity: They conduct electricity when molten or dissolved in water (electrolytes) because ions are free to move, but act as insulators when solid.

💡 Interesting Facts

The electrostatic force holding an ionic lattice together acts in all directions, which is why they form beautiful geometric crystals like table salt cubes.

Total charge is always zero. If Magnesium (Mg²⁺) bonds with Chlorine (Cl⁻), you need two chlorine atoms to balance one magnesium (MgCl₂).

⚠️ Common Mistakes

"Molecules": Ionic compounds do NOT form molecules. They form continuous giant lattices. We use empirical formulas (like NaCl) to show the ratio, not a discrete single entity.

Sharing vs Transfer: Confusing ionic (transfer) with covalent (sharing). Ionic only happens when electronegativity difference is large (>1.7).
