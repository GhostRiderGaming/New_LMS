Transpiration in Plants

The invisible engine driving water upward, defying gravity.

Nature's Hydraulic System

Imagine a pump capable of pulling water hundreds of feet into the air without any moving parts or electricity. That is Transpiration.

It is the process by which moisture is carried through plants from roots to small pores on the underside of leaves, where it changes to vapor and is released to the atmosphere.

Remarkably, only about 1% of the water absorbed by roots is used for growth and photosynthesis. The remaining 99% is lost through transpiration, cooling the plant and acting as a conveyor belt for vital minerals.

Cooling System

Just as sweating cools humans, evaporation of water from leaves cools the plant down, preventing enzymes from denaturing under hot sun.

🌲

Massive Scale

A single large oak tree can transpire over 40,000 gallons (151,000 liters) of water into the atmosphere in a single year!

❌

Common Misconception

People think roots "pump" water up actively. Actually, the evaporation at the leaves creates a vacuum-like pull (tension) that drags the water up passively.
