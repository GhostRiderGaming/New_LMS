Electrolysis Lab

Explore the phenomenon of breaking chemical bonds using electricity. Witness the separation of water into its fundamental gases: Hydrogen and Oxygen.

Apparatus & Components

A standard setup requires a DC power source, conductive electrodes, and an electrolyte solution. Hover or tap the markers to identify each part.

Cathode (Negative)

3

Anode (Positive)

Attracts negative ions ($OH^-$). Site of oxidation where Oxygen gas forms.

4

Electrolyte (Water + Acid)

Pure water doesn't conduct well. A few drops of acid allow ions to move freely.

5

Gas Collection Tubes

Inverted tubes capture rising gases. Note: Hydrogen volume is exactly 2x Oxygen.

Mechanism of Action

Electrolysis is a Continuous Flow process. Electricity drives a continuous loop of electron transfer and ion migration. Step through the stages to see how bonds break.

1 Power Application

The DC battery creates a voltage difference. Electrons are pushed down the wire toward the Cathode, making it negatively charged.

🎯 Practical Applications

  • Electroplating: Coating cheap metals with gold or silver.
  • Extraction: Refining reactive metals like Aluminum from ore.
  • Green Energy: Producing pure Hydrogen fuel from water.

💡 Interesting Fact

Because water is H₂O, splitting it always produces exactly twice as much Hydrogen gas as Oxygen gas by volume. You can literally see the chemical formula in the test tubes!

⚠️ Common Mistakes

Students often mix up the poles. Remember: Cations (positive) go to the Cathode (negative). Anions (negative) go to the Anode (positive). Opposites attract!

🧠 Quick Revision

Remember the acronym OIL RIG:

  • Oxidation Is Loss (of electrons) → occurs at Anode.
  • Reduction Is Gain (of electrons) → occurs at Cathode.
