Energy in Motion

From atomic vibrations to electromagnetic waves.

1. Conduction

The transfer of heat through direct contact within a solid material. Heat causes atoms to vibrate rapidly, transferring kinetic energy to adjacent, cooler atoms through continuous collisions.

Anatomy of Conduction

Working Mechanism: Molecular Scale

Stage 1/4
Thermal Equilibrium
Atoms vibrate slightly at ambient temperature. No net heat flow occurs.

2. Convection

The transfer of heat by the macroscopic movement of a fluid (liquid or gas). As fluid heats up, it expands, becomes less dense, and rises, while cooler, denser fluid sinks to replace it, creating a continuous cycle.

Convection Cell Architecture

Working Mechanism: Fluid Dynamics

Stage 1/4
Heat Application
Energy transfers to the bottom fluid layer.

3. Radiation

The transfer of energy via electromagnetic waves. Unlike conduction and convection, radiation requires no physical medium and can travel across the vacuum of space at the speed of light.

Electromagnetic Emission

Working Mechanism: Wave Propagation

Stage 1/3
Photon Emission
Thermal energy agitates electrons, emitting infrared photons.

Common Misconceptions

❌ "Cold flows into the room."
✅ Cold is just the absence of heat. Heat always flows from high to low energy. You lose heat to the outside.
❌ "Heat rises."
✅ Hot fluids rise due to buoyancy (convection). Heat itself transfers in all directions.
