How It Works: Le Chatelier's Principle

Indicators are weak acids (HIn). They change shape—and therefore color—when they lose or gain a hydrogen ion (H⁺).

H⁺
In⁻

Stage 1: Acidic Environment

In an acid, excess H⁺ ions surround the indicator. The equilibrium shifts left, forcing the indicator to keep its H⁺ attached. The intact molecule (HIn) displays Color A.

Equation: HIn (Color A) ⇌ H⁺ + In⁻ (Color B)

Context & Applications

Titration

Titration

Indicators are vital in titrations. By noting the exact moment the liquid changes color (the endpoint), chemists can calculate the exact concentration of an unknown acid or base.

Universal Indicator

Natural Indicators

You can make your own! Red cabbage juice contains anthocyanins that act as a natural universal indicator, turning red in acid, blue in neutral, and green/yellow in bases.

Common Mistake

Adding Too Much

A common lab error is adding too much indicator. Since indicators are themselves weak acids/bases, adding too much can actually shift the pH of the solution you are trying to test.
