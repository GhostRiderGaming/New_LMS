Thales's Theorem Laboratory

Definition

Thales's Theorem states that if A, B, and C are distinct points on a circle where the line AC is a diameter, then the angle ∠ABC is always a right angle (90°).

This fundamental principle of geometry proves that any triangle inscribed in a semicircle is a right-angled triangle.

Mathematical Proof

∠ABC = 90° = π2 rad
AB2 + BC2 = AC2
Bx = R · cos(θ), By = R · sin(θ)

💡 Historical Fact

Named after Thales of Miletus (c. 624 – c. 546 BC), an ancient Greek philosopher. He is often credited as the first person to use deductive reasoning applied to geometry.
