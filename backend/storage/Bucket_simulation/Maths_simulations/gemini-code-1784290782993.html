📘 Definition & Theory

An algebraic expression is a mathematical phrase containing numbers, variables (like x), and arithmetic operators (+, -, ×, ÷).

Unlike an equation, an expression does not have an equals sign. By evaluating an expression—substituting specific numerical values for the variables—we generate outputs that map curves on a graph.

Standard Polynomial Forms

Linear: y = mx + b
Quadratic: y = ax2 + bx + c
Cubic: y = ax3 + bx2 + cx + d

💡 Did You Know?

The word "Algebra" comes from the Arabic term "al-jabr", meaning "reunion of broken parts".

It was coined in the 9th century by Persian mathematician and astronomer Al-Khwarizmi in his monumental book on calculation.
