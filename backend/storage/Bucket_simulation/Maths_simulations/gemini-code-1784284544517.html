Number Theory Prime Factorization

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every integer greater than 1 either is a prime number itself or can be represented uniquely as a product of prime numbers, disregarding the order of factors.

Mathematical Formulation

For any integer N > 1, it can be factored as:

N = p1a1 · p2a2 · ... · pkak

Where pi are distinct prime numbers (e.g., 2, 3, 5, 7...) and ai are positive integers representing their frequencies.

💡

Prime numbers are the fundamental "building blocks" (atoms) of all integers. The uniqueness of this factorization is what makes primes so crucial in modern cryptography!
