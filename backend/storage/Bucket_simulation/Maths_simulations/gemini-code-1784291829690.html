🔬 Concept Definition

Euclidean geometry is a mathematical system attributed to Alexandrian Greek mathematician Euclid (c. 300 BC). It is based on a small set of intuitively appealing axioms, deducing many theorems from them.

In a flat, two-dimensional Euclidean plane, the shortest path between two points is a straight line, and the interior angles of a planar triangle always sum to exactly 180 degrees.

📐 Core Formulas

d(A, B) = √(x2 - x1)2 + (y2 - y1)2
Area = √s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)

💡 Random Fact

Euclid's Elements is one of the most influential books in history. It served as the primary textbook for teaching mathematics from the time of its publication (around 300 BC) until the late 19th or early 20th century!
